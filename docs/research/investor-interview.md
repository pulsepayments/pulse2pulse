## What this changes (implications for the plan)
1. **Persona = high-net-worth / investor treasury, not retail remittance.** The payout rail is **bank only**. Mobile money is irrelevant for this segment: it caps at ZMW 20k per transaction, ≈ £740.
2. **Ticket sizes break the provisional off-ramp.** Yellow Card bank payout tops out at ~ZMW 1M (≈ £37k) per transaction, if that figure is a limit. A £500k ticket would mean ~14 splits. We need **OTC / large-ticket liquidity** partners and written per-transaction and daily limits in the £100k–£3M range.
3. **ZMW liquidity depth is a real risk** at £1M+. Explore **paying out USD into the recipient's own Zambian USD (domiciliary) account**. That removes the kwacha conversion from the critical path. *Hypothesis: confirm with Zambian counsel against the Currency Directives 2025 (cross-border receipts appear exempt) and with partners.*
4. **Own-account (first-party) transfers** simplify the risk picture: sender = beneficiary, and source of funds is documented once. KYB/KYC still needs **enhanced due diligence** at these amounts, and investors may send from **company** accounts (KYB, UBO).
5. **Competitive gap is real at this size.** Consumer apps cap far lower (e.g. Western Union ~£4.8k; WorldRemit mobile-money caps). The comparison is a **bank SWIFT wire or an FX broker**, not Wise or Revolut retail. Still benchmark Revolut Business and a UK FX broker in 0b.
6. **Unit economics flip.** At £100k+, percentage fees are big money. Price as a **tight FX spread plus a small fixed fee**, tiered by band. Aim to beat the SWIFT FX margin, not the flat wire fee.
7. **Virtual accounts for everyone:** named USD/GBP/EUR accounts for senders (fund once, send many) and receiving accounts for Zambian users. Still after Send (Phase B), fiat-settled.
